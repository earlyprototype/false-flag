## Summary

Irish Manufacturing Research (IMR) is a not-for-profit, industry-led Research & Technology Organisation (RTO) founded in 2014, now employing 120+ people across two Irish sites (Rathcoole, Co. Dublin and Mullingar, Co. Westmeath). It operates four research pillars — Digitisation, Robotics & Automation, Design for Manufacturing, and Sustainable Manufacturing — and coordinates Ireland's national European Digital Innovation Hub (FactoryXChange), the CIRCULÉIRE circular-economy network, and the ESA Phi-Lab Ireland. IMR's digital-twin work is concentrated in the €6.9M Horizon Europe REWIRE project (2026–2030), which it coordinates, and in its FactoryXChange test-before-invest services. Senior technical staff publishing on digital twins and Industry 4.0 include CTO Micheal Cassidy, Director of Digitalisation David Keeley, Robotics Technologist Dr Aswin Ramasubramanian (REWIRE Coordinator), and Senior XR Researcher Eoghan Kidney. IMR is also a regional hub partner for the 2026 TechIreland National AI Challenge (28 Aug – 14 Sep 2026), which focuses on AI agents and agentic workflows.

## 1. Organisation Overview

Irish Manufacturing Research (IMR) is an independent, not-for-profit Research & Technology Organisation established in 2014 to support the Irish manufacturing sector through applied research, collaborative pilots, and knowledge transfer [1]. The organisation describes its mission as helping organisations "make better decisions, build stronger capabilities, and achieve progress" through practical innovation, with a vision of enabling organisations to "digitise, automate and scale with confidence" [1].

IMR operates from two locations: a Rathcoole office at Unit A, Aerodrome Business Park, Rathcoole, Co. Dublin (D24 WC04), and the IMR AM Lab at the National Science Park, Dublin Road, Mullingar, Co. Westmeath (N91 TX80) [1]. The organisation has grown from two employees at founding to more than 120 people working across Ireland and Europe [1].

## 3. Key Projects & Initiatives

### 3.1 REWIRE (Horizon Europe, 2026–2030)

REWIRE is a €6.9 million Horizon Europe project coordinated by IMR, running from May 2026 to April 2030 [3] [4]. The project aims to scale high-quality remanufacturing across Europe through robotics, AI, digital twins, and traceability, targeting the heavy machinery, automotive, and electronics sectors [3].

The consortium comprises 13 partners from 8 countries: 3 Research & Technology Organisations, 4 Research Performing Organisations, 1 NGO, and 5 industrial partners [3]. Irish partners include IMR (Coordinator), Dromone Engineering Ltd., and University College Dublin [3]. Other partners include the University of Genoa and Centro Ricerche Fiat (Stellantis) from Italy, the University of Patras Laboratory for Manufacturing Systems and Automation from Greece, IDEKO S. Coop and PILZ Industrieelektronik SL from Spain, Danfoss Power Electronics A/S from Denmark, Linz Center for Mechatronics GmbH from Austria, Netcompany S.A. from Luxembourg, and the University of Kragujevac Faculty of Engineering from Serbia [3].

Key technologies include digital twins, Digital Product Passports (DPPs), FAIR data principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable), modular robotics, AI planners, and predictive safety architectures [3]. The project aims to double remanufactured component volumes, reduce waste and CO2 emissions, and build resilient supply chains [3].

**Grant details:** Grant agreement 101293592; total cost €7,665,567.50; EU contribution €6,998,837.38; IMR's net EU contribution €1,514,521.25 [4].

**Confidence:** High — sourced from Horizon Europe official project page and CORDIS database.

### 3.2 FactoryXChange (European Digital Innovation Hub)

FactoryXChange (FXC) is Ireland's national European Digital Innovation Hub (EDIH) for advanced manufacturing, led by IMR and co-funded by the European Commission and Enterprise Ireland [5] [6]. Phase 2 funding of €23 million was announced in January 2026 [7].

FXC partners include Technological University of the Shannon (TUS), Irish Centre for High-End Computing (ICHEC), KPMG Future Analytics, INSIGHT SFI Research Centre, and Creative Spark [5]. Services include Digital Maturity Assessments, Innovation Roadmapping, Technical Mentoring, and Prototype & Feasibility Trials [5].

As of July 2025, FactoryXChange had delivered 589 services booked by Irish SMEs and public sector bodies, supported 257 companies directly, completed 186 Digital Maturity Assessments, delivered €4.75 million in services, unlocked €3.66 million in follow-on investment, achieved 91.4% customer satisfaction, and recorded a Net Promoter Score of 73.6 [5].

The programme supports companies adopting AI, robotics, data analytics, and digital twins, with a focus on manufacturing SMEs, startups, and public sector organisations [5] [6].

**Confidence:** High — sourced from IMR case study and FactoryXChange official website.

### 3.3 CIRCULÉIRE

CIRCULÉIRE is Ireland's first national circular economy innovation network, coordinated by IMR and funded by the Department of Environment, Climate and Communications and the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) [8]. The network supports circular economy projects, innovation funding, and cross-sector collaboration, with more than 50 industry members [8].

**Confidence:** High — sourced from IMR projects page.

### 3.4 ESA Phi-Lab Ireland

ESA Phi-Lab Ireland is part of the European Space Agency's Phi-Lab Network, established through a partnership between IMR and the AMBER Research Centre at Trinity College Dublin [9]. The lab focuses on advanced materials and manufacturing for space-optimised hardware, offering seed funding of €200,000–€400,000 per project [9]. The first Open Call launched in 2025 [9].

**Confidence:** High — sourced from IMR news and projects pages.

### 3.5 DigiWind

DigiWind is an EU co-funded project under the Digital Europe Programme, focused on closing the digital skills gap in the renewable energy sector [10]. IMR is one of five country partners, delivering Masters degrees, self-paced online masters, lifelong learning modules, and a virtual campus [10].

**Confidence:** High — sourced from IMR projects page.

### 3.6 DATUM

DATUM is a cross-border digital transformation programme for manufacturing SMEs, delivered in partnership between IMR and the Advanced Manufacturing Innovation Centre at Queen's University Belfast [11]. The programme is funded through PEACEPLUS via the Special EU Programmes Body (SEUPB) and follows an Assess (using SIRI — Smart Industry Readiness Index), Plan, Transform pathway [11].

**Confidence:** High — sourced from IMR news.

### 3.7 CoreSense

CoreSense is an EU project focused on developing a cognitive architecture for autonomous robots, addressing understanding, awareness, resilience, and explainability [12].

**Confidence:** High — sourced from IMR news.

## 7. Digital Twin Work

IMR's digital twin work is concentrated in three areas:

### 7.1 REWIRE Project

REWIRE uses digital twins for remanufacturing workflows, implementing Digital Product Passports (DPPs) for lifecycle data, model-based systems engineering, and real-time, data-driven decisions [3]. The project applies FAIR data principles and develops predictive safety architectures and adaptive AI planners for automated disassembly and inspection [3].

### 7.2 FactoryXChange Services

FactoryXChange supports companies adopting digital twins through Prototype & Feasibility Testing in Real or Simulated Environments [5]. Digital Maturity Assessments include digital twin readiness evaluation [5].

### 7.3 Mixed Reality / XR

IMR's mixed reality work, led by Senior XR Researcher Eoghan Kidney, focuses on immersive digital twins, real-time instruction, and intuitive human-machine interaction [16]. The organisation showcased XR devices from enterprise headsets to emerging AI-powered smart glasses at its November 2025 Industry Members Network Event [16].

## 8. 2026 TechIreland National AI Challenge

### 8.1 Overview

The TechIreland National AI Challenge 2026 is a two-week innovation challenge running from 28 August to 14 September 2026, coordinated by TechIreland in partnership with Enterprise Ireland and the European Digital Innovation Hubs Network [22] [23]. Now in its third year, the challenge brings together developers, engineers, founders, students, researchers, AI enthusiasts, and business professionals from across Ireland and Northern Ireland to build AI-powered solutions to real-world problems [22].

More than 300 participants are expected, with over 200 registering within the first eight days [23]. Participation is free [22].

### 8.2 Theme

The 2026 theme is **"AI & Agents: Strategy, Processes and Roles,"** reflecting the shift from AI experimentation to practical implementation [22] [23]. The challenge focuses on AI agents and agentic workflows across sectors including manufacturing, healthcare, sustainability, financial services, insurance, public services, enterprise technology, and immersive technology [22].

Teams can respond to industry-led Challenge Statements or submit wildcard ideas [22]. The programme emphasises responsible, people-centred AI, asking participants to consider not only what can be built, but also how it can be implemented, governed, and used to create meaningful impact [23].

## 10. Relevance to FALSE FLAG

IMR's digital twin expertise, particularly in the REWIRE project and FactoryXChange services, aligns directly with the Situation Globe sprint's requirements for real-time, data-driven decision support and immersive visualisation. The organisation's focus on AI agents and agentic workflows in the National AI Challenge mirrors the game's LLM-driven advisor system.

IMR's membership model and network of 80+ manufacturing companies could provide validation and pilot opportunities for FALSE FLAG's crisis simulation mechanics, particularly if the game is positioned as a training or decision-support tool for industrial or government contexts.

The National AI Challenge's emphasis on responsible, people-centred AI and governance aligns with FALSE FLAG's exploration of manufactured geopolitical incidents and the ethical implications of AI-driven decision-making.
