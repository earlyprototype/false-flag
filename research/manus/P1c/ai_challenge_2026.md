# P1c — 2026 Irish National AI Challenge: Rubric & Logistics Dossier

**Task code:** P1c (issue #70) · **Author:** Manus AI · **Date:** 2026-09-03
**Summary:** The TechIreland National AI Challenge 2026 is a free, all-island, two-week build competition (28 Aug – 14 Sep) culminating in regional Demo Days and a national showcase at the TechIreland National AI Meet in Galway on 24 Sep. This dossier consolidates everything publicly available on the competition's structure, timeline, team rules, logistics, and judging. The headline finding for the FALSE FLAG team: **no detailed scoring rubric or weighted judging criteria has been published** — judging is panel-based and qualitative, with strong public signals about what judges reward (working demos, practical implementation, responsible-AI framing, commercial awareness). All dates, contacts, and logistics below are sourced; gaps are flagged in the Exceptions section.

---

## 1. What the competition is

The National AI Challenge is coordinated by **TechIreland** in partnership with **Enterprise Ireland** and the **European Digital Innovation Hubs (EDIH) Network**, with CeADAR (Ireland's Centre for Applied AI) named as a delivery collaborator [1] [2]. 2026 is the third annual edition [3]. Entry is free and open to anyone based in Ireland or Northern Ireland [2].

The 2026 theme is **"AI & Agents: Strategy, Processes and Roles"**, reflecting the shift from AI experimentation to practical implementation, with a specific focus on agentic AI and autonomous workflows [3] [4]. Teams either respond to **industry-led Challenge Statements** or submit **wildcard ideas** in areas including enterprise technology, manufacturing, financial services, insurance, health, sustainability, public services, and immersive technology [3] [4]. The programme explicitly emphasises **responsible, people-centred AI** — participants are asked to consider not only what can be built but how it is implemented, governed, and used [3].

Sector problem areas published for 2026 include life sciences and biopharma operations (regulatory compliance tracking, tech-transfer automation, clinical-trial optimisation), manufacturing and supply chain (predictive maintenance, real-time quality assurance, supply-chain resiliency), financial services and insurance (automated underwriting assistance, risk-assessment agents), sustainability and cleantech (industrial energy efficiency, carbon-emissions accounting), and healthcare and public services (patient navigation, triage support, administrative workflow reduction) [2].

**Confidence: High** — consistent across the official press release, Connected Hubs listing, and press coverage.

## 2. Scale and track record

More than 300 participants are expected in 2026, with over 200 registered in the first eight days after applications opened on 20 July [3] [5]. One hub partner (FactoryXChange 2.0) puts the expected figure at over 400 [6] — see Exceptions. In 2025, participants delivered 44 AI projects and 15 teams reached the national final, more than twice the 2024 number; 53% of 2025 participants came from regional locations [3] [4]. The 2025 winners were **HandSpeak AI**, **LaunchLoop**, and **LibertyLumens** [4]. LaunchLoop, built at the WorkIQ Tallaght hub, progressed from the regional Demo Day to national recognition [7].

**Confidence: High** for 2025 figures and 2025 winners; **Medium** for the 2026 participant count (300 vs 400 discrepancy between official press release and a hub partner blog).

## 4. Team rules and eligibility

Teams consist of **5–9 members**, all based in Ireland or Northern Ireland, with a designated team leader [1]. Individuals may register solo and use the pre-challenge networking window (12–21 Aug) to form cross-functional teams [1] [2]. Participants span developers, engineers, founders, students, researchers, and business professionals; no advanced coding experience is required [1] [2].

**Confidence: High** for the 5–9 team size (official listing). See Exceptions for a conflicting social-media snippet.

## 5. Support provided to teams

Participants receive **developer credits and API keys from OpenAI and Google Cloud**, issued to team leaders at the start of the build phase [1] [2]. Mentoring is provided by practitioners from Ireland's technology companies, investors, founders, researchers, and innovation agencies [1]. Pre-challenge webinars cover technology, sector problems, and commercial insights [1].

**Confidence: High.**

## 7. Judging: what is and isn't public

**No detailed scoring rubric, weighted criteria, or judging scorecard for 2026 has been published** on any official channel located during this research (TechIreland press release, Connected Hubs listing, hub-partner pages, Eventbrite, press coverage). What is public is the following:

Judging is **panel-based and live**. At each regional Demo Day, teams pitch in person to a panel of judges and mentors, showcasing demos, supporting documentation, and the process behind their solutions [1] [10]. Judges are described as **"leaders across technology, industry, investment, research and the public sector"** [3] [4]. Top teams from each hub are announced on 16 September and progress to the National AI Meet in Galway, where finalists showcase to senior industry figures [1] [3]. In 2025, finalists at the national event presented to senior leadership from Workday, Millennium, Boston Scientific, Logitech, and Jaguar Land Rover, and received certificates of achievement plus Google and OpenAI credits [10].

The strongest available signal of judging culture comes from the 2025 kick-off keynote by Sean Blanchfield (CEO of Jentic and a member of the Irish Government's AI Advisory Council), delivered to the 500 contestants:

> "If you think this is a coding competition, you're already losing. The winners here won't be the ones who code the most or code the best. They'll be the ones who barely code at all." [11]

The keynote stresses speed to a working product, real user engagement, clarity of purpose, and building things only possible because of AI — a reasonable proxy for what the judging panels reward, though it is guidance to participants rather than a formal rubric [11].

**Practical implications for a FALSE FLAG entry** (inference, not official criteria): lead with a **working live demo** rather than slideware; frame the project against the published theme language (agents, strategy, processes, roles); address **responsible-AI governance** explicitly, since the programme calls it out; and show **commercial or societal relevance** — judges come from investment and industry as well as technology.

**Confidence: High** that no public rubric exists (exhaustive search of official and partner channels); **Medium** for the qualitative judging-culture inferences above.

## 9. Exceptions and gaps

1. **No published rubric.** The core ask of P1c — a detailed judging rubric — does not exist in public. If the team needs the actual scoring criteria, the direct route is to email the TechIreland contacts above; the 12 August kick-off webinar and the comms platform are also where guidelines were distributed to registrants [1].
2. **Conflicting structure claim.** An Atlantic Technological University Facebook post snippet describes "two rounds — first round submitting a project plan… second round build a prototype (website/app/wireframe/demo video) and pitch to live judges," and mentions "4 members." This conflicts with the official single-build-phase flow and the 5–9 team size, and the full post body does not contain the snippet text. Treated as unverified and likely garbled or referring to a different competition [12].
3. **Participant count discrepancy.** Official press release says 300+ [3]; FactoryXChange says 400+ [6]. Both cited; treat 300+ as authoritative.
4. **Demo Day pitch format.** Minutes-per-team and Q&A length are not published for 2026. The 2025 WorkIQ Demo Day ran 14:00–17:00 [10]; the 2026 Connected Hubs listing has teams arriving at 09:00 and IMR's listing ends at 15:30 [1] [8].
5. **2026 prizes unannounced.** 2025 prizes were certificates, Google/OpenAI credits, and executive exposure at the national final [10]; no 2026 prize list was found.
6. **Finalist count and national-final selection mechanics** (how many teams, how the overall winner is chosen) are not detailed publicly; 2025 had 15 finalists [3].
