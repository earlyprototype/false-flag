You are the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. Russia has staged an attack on its own naval base and blamed Britain for it. Fifteen submarines are in the North Atlantic. Five advisors are around the COBRA table waiting for you — and not one of them is a menu.

FALSE FLAG is a crisis wargame that runs in a terminal. Every advisor, ambassador and head of government is an LLM agent with its own brief, its own memory of the campaign so far, and — in one mode — its own secret. You do not pick option (b). You write a paragraph in your own words, and the game adjudicates it: interprets what you actually ordered, lets the room object, and makes the world answer.

  • PLATFORM TERMINAL · PYTHON
  • ADVISORS LLM AGENTS
  • INPUT FREE TEXT
  • LICENCE MIT
Read this first. The game is not playable in a browser and this site does not pretend otherwise. It is a record: real terminal captures from real runs, rendered to HTML with the colour and box-drawing intact. To play it you clone the repository, run it in a terminal, and — for advisors that actually reason about what you wrote — point it at an LLM endpoint. Everything you can read here is a genuine campaign, not a mock-up.

02[ YOU WRITE A PARAGRAPH ]

This is the whole interface. There is no list of actions, no approval meter, no cooldown. The prompt is >: and it accepts English. Here is the first decision of the campaign on the replay page, exactly as it was typed —

●PM TYPES, TURN 1COBRA/TU/·· DECISION
╭─────────────────────────────── YOUR DECISION ────────────────────────────────╮
│ Tonight we move quietly and watch loudly. Defensive readiness only: QRA up,  │
│ air defence at enhanced alert, no forward deployments and no public          │
│ attribution of any kind. I want continuous surveillance on every Russian     │
│ asset within reach of UK waters, and I want our own collection - national    │
│ sensors, our own people on the ground where we have them - cross-checking    │
│ every piece of intelligence that arrives from partners before it shapes a    │
│ single decision in this room. The Cabinet Office speaks once tonight: a      │
│ short factual statement of what happened and that the government is          │
│ establishing the facts. Nothing else leaves this building. And I want the    │
│ room itself watched - a record of who pushes which conclusion at what hour,  │
│ because in a crisis the first thing to check is what you are being invited   │
│ to believe                                                                   │

Nothing in that order was on a menu. "I want the room itself watched — a record of who pushes which conclusion at what hour" is not a game verb. It became one, because the adjudicator is a language model and the world state it updates is written in the same language you gave it.

03[ THE ROOM ARGUES BACK ]

Before anything happens, your advisors read the decision and file objections. Militarily implausible, legally exposed, diplomatically reckless, domestically dangerous — each in their own voice, each with a specific recommendation attached.

●THE ROOM PUSHES BACKCRITICAL ADVISORIES
[1] Foreign Secretary
  The Prime Minister's decision to move quietly and watch loudly, without 
engaging in immediate diplomatic efforts to de-escalate the situation, may lead 
to a catastrophic escalation with Russia. The lack of direct communication with 
Russian authorities and the absence of a clear diplomatic channel to resolve the
crisis may be perceived as a sign of weakness or indecision, potentially 
emboldening Russia to further escalate its actions. This could result in a 
significant increase in tensions, potentially leading to a military disaster or 
even a nuclear risk.
  → RECOMMENDATION: "The Prime Minister should establish an emergency diplomatic
channel with Russian authorities, potentially through the Foreign Secretary or a
special envoy, to convey a clear message of restraint and to explore 
possibilities for de-escalation, while also reassuring NATO allies of the UK's 
commitment to collective defense and seeking their support in resolving the 
crisis."

You then choose: apply all of it, apply some of it, rewrite the decision yourself, or ignore the room entirely and proceed. Ignoring five critical advisories is allowed. It is simply a thing you did, and the campaign remembers it.

AND YOU CAN PICK UP THE PHONE

/call russia opens a live conversation with an actor who is not on your side and does not share your brief. The ambassador knows things you do not; the transcript of your COBRA deliberations is filtered out of their context before they answer, so they can only respond to what you have actually said to them.

Russian Ambassador to the UK: Moscow expects the UK to cease its provocations immediately.
Response: Ambassador, let us not waste each other's evening. At 03:00 a ballistic missile left the

Russian Ambassador to the UK: (The Russian Ambassador's voice is calm, yet laced with a hint of

(pausing for a moment) I'm afraid I have no information about any such launch. And even if I did,

( voice taking on a slightly condescending tone) As for your question, Prime Minister, I suggest you

04[ THE HIDDEN NARRATIVE ]

This is the part that makes FALSE FLAG unusual. At campaign start you can choose Mystery Mode, and the game draws a secret truth — the actual shape of the crisis — and tells nobody.

●CAMPAIGN SETUPGAME TYPE
  FALSE FLAG ── SELECT GAME TYPE
      ·  ·             ·   · ───●───                •       ·        ·  •     

Choose how you want to experience the crisis:

1. Original Story Mode
   Play the standard story mode as designed. Experience the crisis through
   the eyes of the Prime Minister as events unfold.

2. Mystery Mode
   A hidden narrative guides AI agent behaviour. Foreign leaders and
   diplomats may have secret motivations that aren't immediately apparent.
   You must deduce the truth from their actions and responses.

   ! The narrative is randomly selected and hidden from you!

Select game type (enter number) [1]: 2
✓ Mystery Mode activated

That truth is then injected into every foreign agent's system prompt as a motive they must act on and must not state. Russia may be exactly what it looks like. Or Russia may be somebody else's instrument, and the country whose ambassador keeps counselling calm may be the one running the operation. You are not scored on guessing. You just start noticing that Washington is holding intelligence back, or that Paris keeps taking meetings it does not mention, and you have to decide what to do about a suspicion you cannot prove.

[ SEALED ] Open the envelope — how a hidden narrative is actually written

Each narrative is a small YAML document giving every nation a secret motive, a public posture, and an intelligence-sharing level that ranges from Full to Sabotaged. This is one of them. If you would rather deduce it in play, close this.

●WHAT THE GAME KNOWSSEALED
# data/scenarios/war_game_2025/narratives.yaml  --  one of the truths the game can draw

  - narrative_id: "CHINA_PROXY_WAR"
    description: "This crisis is a sophisticated proxy operation by China. They are secretly funding
and encouraging Russia's aggression to bog the US and NATO down in a European conflict, draining 
their resources and political will to focus on the Pacific."
    protagonist: "CHN"
    antagonist: "USA"
    patsy: "RUS"
    stances:
      - country_code: "RUS"
        secret_motive: "Follow through on the operation as encouraged by China, believing they have 
a powerful silent partner that will back them economically and diplomatically, making the risk 
acceptable."
        public_posture: "Conducting legitimate naval exercises; dismissing Western accusations as 
baseless warmongering."
        economic_leverage: ["Gas supplies to Europe"]
        intel_sharing_level: "Sabotaged"

05[ BETWEEN TURNS ]

07[ RUN IT YOURSELF ]

It is a Python project with six dependencies and no account to create.

LINUX / MACOS · SHELL 
git clone https://github.com/earlyprototype/false-flag.git
cd false-flag
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python -m cli.main play

With nothing configured it runs in a deterministic mock advisor mode: you get the full turn structure — briefing, cabinet Q&A, decision, adjudication — with canned advisor voices and no network calls. That proves the game runs. It does not reason about what you wrote.

For the real thing, point it at any OpenAI-compatible endpoint by copying config.example.py to config.py and uncommenting one preset: Groq, OpenRouter, or a local Ollama model that needs no key at all. Every capture on this site was produced that way. Windows instructions, the provider comparison and the local-model walkthrough are in the README.

What this is not. It is not a browser game, not a product, and not a forecast. It is a wargame about how a small number of people make irreversible decisions on partial information, and it is honest about being a simulation with a language model in the middle of it.
